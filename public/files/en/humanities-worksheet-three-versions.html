Humanities worksheet: how our community has changed — three versions

One Humanities worksheet, three versions: standard, scaffolded and visual · BEETS AI · Theme E · #6 · v0.1 (draft)

⚠️ The neighbourhood "On Wo Estate" and all the people, places and dates on this page are fictional. This document is a draft and must be reviewed by an educator before publication.

How to use

  1. First, read the two passages below with the class (or read them aloud).
  2. Give each pupil one of the three versions — standard, scaffolded or visual — according to need.
  3. All three versions aim at the same learning objective and can run in the same classroom at once.
  4. The teacher note (the shaded box under each version) explains what changed and why.

Reading (common to all three versions — a fictional example)

On Wo Estate, forty years ago

Forty years ago, On Wo Estate had only low wooden huts and market stalls. Residents queued at the communal standpipe on the corner to fetch water. To cross the harbour, people could only take the ferry, and the trip there and back took a long time. There was no MTR station and no big shopping centre. The Tin Hau temple stood at the entrance to the village, with open ground in front where the children used to play.

On Wo Estate today

Today, On Wo Estate has become high-rise blocks, and every household has piped water. There is a supermarket and an MTR station next to the estate, so crossing the harbour is quick. A large shopping centre has replaced some of the market stalls, but there is still a market at the entrance to the estate. The Tin Hau temple is still there, and the open ground in front has become a park with a children's playground and fitness equipment for the elderly.

Version 1 — Standard

Standard

Task: Read the two passages carefully, then complete the questions.

1. Complete the table (at least two items in each column):

ThenNowBoth then and now

2. Choose one change from the table and write the change and one possible reason for it.

3. What do you think is most worth keeping in On Wo Estate? Why?

Teacher note: Keep full reading and open writing to practise comparing, sorting and reasoning. The questions use open wording so more able pupils organise their own answers without frames or a word bank.

Version 2 — Scaffolded

Scaffolded

Step 1: Circle. In the word bank, circle the "then" items in one colour and the "now" items in another.

Word bank: wooden huts · high-rise blocks · market stalls · supermarket · ferry · MTR station · communal standpipe · piped water · Tin Hau temple · market

Step 2: Fill in the blanks using words from the word bank.

Then there were ______, now there are ______.

Then people fetched ______, now there is ______.

Then people took the ______ across the harbour, now they take the ______.

But ______ and ______ existed both then and now.

Step 3: Circle the correct answer. Why do people take the MTR now instead of the ferry?

A. The population grew, so faster transport was needed.    B. People stopped liking boats.

Step 4: What would you keep? Circle one, then write a sentence using the frame.

Tin Hau temple / market / park

"I would keep ______, because ______."

Teacher note: Add sentence frames, a word bank, chunked steps and fewer items, and turn open writing into fill-in and multiple choice, so pupils can finish and succeed. The "reason for the change" becomes a multiple-choice question to lighten the writing load while keeping the objective of giving a reason.

Answer key and marking notes (for teachers)

ItemNotes
Sorting tableThen: wooden huts, market stalls, communal standpipe, ferry, the open ground by the temple; Now: high-rise blocks, piped water, supermarket, MTR station, park / playground; Both: market, Tin Hau temple. Pupils are on track if they name most of these.
Scaffolded fill-inswooden huts → high-rise blocks; standpipe → piped water; ferry → MTR station; market and Tin Hau temple (both then and now).
Reason for the changeScaffolded multiple choice: correct answer A. The standard and visual versions ask this as an open question.
Open writingA good answer contains "one change + one reason", e.g. "People used to take the ferry, but now there is an MTR station, because the population grew and faster transport was needed." 1 mark for the change, 1 more for a plausible reason.
Worth keepingA good answer contains "one feature + one reason", e.g. "Keep the Tin Hau temple, because it is part of the community's history and the elders have memories of it."
Visual sort"Both then and now" should be: market, Tin Hau temple. If a pupil places "market" only under "then", point them to "there is still a market at the entrance to the estate".
