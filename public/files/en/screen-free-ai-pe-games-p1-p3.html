P1-P3 PE: two screen-free AI games (no devices needed)

Screen-free AI games for P1-P3 PE · BEETS AI · Theme E · #10 · v0.1 (draft)

⚠️ This page is a draft, to be reviewed by an educator before publication. No device or screen is needed at any point.

Equipment

□ Two cones (one labelled "Uses AI", one labelled "Does not use AI")
□ Two floor mats or hoops (answer zones "Animal" and "Not an animal")
□ Game 1 card set (below — photocopy and cut out)
□ Game 2 card set (below — print two copies of the ball card)
□ (Optional) one whistle

Game 1 — "AI or not AI?"

  1. Place the two cones at opposite ends of the space.
  2. The teacher holds up a card and the class thinks for a second.
  3. The teacher calls "Go!", and pupils run to the cone they think is correct.
  4. The teacher reveals the answer, sends anyone who is wrong to the correct side, and gives a one-sentence reason.
  5. Repeat with 6–8 cards.

Key idea: not everything electronic "uses AI"; only tools that decide for themselves count.

Game 2 — "Train the model"

  1. Teach (about 4 min): one pupil is the "model", standing with their back to the class (or eyes closed). The teacher shows cards one by one (cat, dog, fish → animal; ball, car → not animal); the class says the correct label out loud so the model hears it. Repeat 4–5 cards, all correct.
  2. Test (about 2 min): show a new card (for example "bird"); the class stays silent; the model judges "animal / not animal" and steps into the matching zone.
  3. Add one wrong example (about 2 min): quietly show a "ball" card and signal the class to say "animal" on purpose. Test a new ball card again — the model now judges it as "animal".
  4. Discuss (about 4 min): ask "Why did the model get it wrong?" → one wrong example was mixed into the teaching.

Chant: Right examples, right AI; wrong examples, wrong AI.

Inclusion checklist

□ Pupils who cannot run or move far: point, hold up a coloured card, or be paired so a friend runs on their behalf; shorten the cone distance
□ Visual support: every card carries a large picture plus a word; use large, clear symbols on the cones; say each card aloud
□ Deaf, hard of hearing, or speech difficulty: model every action; allow answers by action or sign
□ Attention support: fewer cards per round and shorter distances

Two-minute check

  1. Does a pencil use AI? — No.
  2. Does a robot vacuum use AI? — Yes (it avoids obstacles by itself).
  3. If I mix one wrong example into the "teaching", what does the model do? — It learns wrong / gets it wrong.
