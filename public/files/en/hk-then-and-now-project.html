Hong Kong then and now: a local history project with an alternative to a family interview

BEETS AI · Theme D · #13 · v0.1 (draft)

⚠️ Every street, place and source on this page is fictional and involves no real family, address or school. This file is a draft and must be reviewed by an educator before publication.

Choose a path

Both paths count and are marked exactly the same. You only need to pick one.

Path A — interview a relative (optional): ask a family member what they remember about how a place used to look.
Path B — public sources (any pupil may choose this): complete the project using street views, old maps, old newspapers or library records. No interview needed.

Path A: interview prompts

  1. Which area did you live in as a child? What were the streets like?
  2. What shops, markets or parks were near your home back then?
  3. How were the buildings there different from today?
  4. Did you visit any particular place there (e.g. a restaurant, a cinema, a pier)? Does it still exist?
  5. What was there when you were young that has now disappeared?
Keep questions to "what was that place like then". Do not ask about names, addresses, occupations or health, and do not record, film or photograph your relative.

Path B: public sources (five steps)

  1. Choose a place near your school or on your daily route (a street, a building, a market).
  2. Walk it (or walk it online): walk the street yourself, or use a map's street view to "walk" it, and note what you see.
  3. Take one photo of a building or a street sign (objects only, no people).
  4. Find one old source: in a library or online, find an old map, an old newspaper or an old photo showing the same place in the past.
  5. Compare then and now: put "what I see today" next to "what the old source shows" and write at least one difference.

Worked source check (fictional)

The task: we check an AI's claim about the past of "Banyan Street".

The AI's claim: "In the 1970s, Banyan Street was a busy street with a market at one end and a cinema in the middle. The cinema closed in 1985 and became a shopping centre."

AI's claimSource checkedResult
There was a market at one endA 1973 old map (fictional)Correct: the map shows a market at one end
There was a cinema in the middleThe same map + an old newspaper (fictional)Partly wrong: the map shows a small open-air theatre, and the newspaper calls it "the Banyan Street theatre"
The cinema closed in 1985 and became a shopping centreA library newspaper index (fictional)Unverified: the index has no record of a 1985 closure
Conclusion: the AI's answer is partly right and partly wrong, so it cannot be trusted without checking it against a source.

Completion checklist

□ I chose a place (a street / a building / a market).
□ I chose a path: A interview or B public sources.
□ I have at least one source (interview notes / photo / map / newspaper / library record).
□ I wrote at least one "then and now" difference.
□ I checked the AI's claim and said right, wrong or not verified.
□ I did not put my family's private details on the sheet.

Teacher notes (please read)

  • Do not ask pupils to share their family's private information aloud (names, addresses, occupations, health).
  • The interview is optional, not required. Any pupil may choose the public-source path, marked identically.
  • Never ask pupils to upload old family photos to an AI tool (or any generative or recognition tool).
  • Do not press, compare or single out pupils who cannot interview family (newly arrived, cross-border, looked-after, bereaved, language barriers).
  • Old photos are used only for classroom display or within agreed parental consent; they are not uploaded to third-party tools.
