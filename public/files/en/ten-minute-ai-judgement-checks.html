Ten-minute AI judgement checks: three formative tasks

BEETS AI · Theme C · #3 · v0.1 (draft)

⚠️ All examples are fictional and do not involve any real pupils or schools. Teachers can swap in content from their own subject. This file is a draft and must be reviewed by an educator before publication.

How to use

  1. Each task takes about 10 minutes and can run as a lesson opener, or all three together in one lesson (about 40 minutes).
  2. Hand out the worksheet. Do not grade whether pupils used AI; score the judgement only.
  3. When you collect the answers, use the scoring guide on the last page to decide what to teach next.

For the teacher: these three tasks do not grade whether pupils used AI. They score judgement — spotting missing evidence, defending understanding, and planning a check.

Scoring guide and answer notes (teacher)

Task 1

LevelWhat it looks like
StrongPoints to "make you smarter" and "eight glasses" as doubtful, and says what evidence is missing (for example "it does not say which studies" or "there is no source for eight glasses").
DevelopingCan tell which parts sound reasonable and which are doubtful, but cannot say what evidence is missing.
Needs helpAccepts or rejects the whole answer without going sentence by sentence.

Follow-up: sort the class's "missing evidence" into three kinds — source, data, example — and post them as a classroom poster.

Task 2

LevelWhat it looks like
StrongCorrectly spots that the first sentence is the writer's own words (plain, close to everyday life) and the second looks copied (technical terms, no explanation), and says why.
DevelopingSpots some of it, but the reason is vague (for example only "this sentence is harder").
Needs helpCannot tell the difference, or thinks the whole answer is either all the writer's own or all copied.

Follow-up: pupils take out their last piece of work and circle the parts they can explain in their own words.

Task 3

LevelWhat it looks like
StrongLists at least three things to check, including sources and facts ("which scientist said this?", "is this fact or guess?"), and can say which one matters most.
DevelopingLists only surface checks (spelling, layout) and misses sources and facts.
Needs helpThinks "finished writing" means "ready to hand in", and cannot name anything to check.

Follow-up: merge the individual checklists into one shared "before-you-hand-in" checklist and post it in the classroom.

How this relates to the EDB framework

This maps to F (the AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools, p.1-13) and G (the Guide to Using AI in Teaching in Primary and Secondary Schools, pp.2-7 to 2-8): F calls for varied, authentic assessment that covers application, ethics and thinking processes; G recommends oral questioning and the submission of thinking processes when checking learning and AI use.
