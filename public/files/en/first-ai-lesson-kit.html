Your first AI lesson: a self-guided kit for P4-P6 teachers who have never taught with AI

BEETS AI · Theme E · #9 · v0.1 (draft)

⚠️ All examples are fictional demonstrations and do not involve any real pupils, schools or cases. Teachers can swap in their own school-verified sources and questions. This file is a draft and must be reviewed by an educator before publication.

1. Preparation checklist

□ Print the pupil worksheet (one per pupil) and the source cards (one per pair)
□ Prepare the 8 slides, or copy Example 1 onto the whiteboard
□ Ask the AI once yourself, check the answer, and decide which question to use for the demonstration
□ Arrange pairs, matching pupils who need support with stronger partners
□ Prepare the "more challenge" extension for pairs that finish early
If the AI tool is unavailable on the day: carry on as planned, using one of these backups —
□ Use the pre-printed AI answers (included in this kit, no internet needed)
□ Or "teacher plays the AI": read a prepared answer aloud to the class, then check it as usual

3. Inclusive lesson design

More support (for pupils with learning needs):
□ The source cards have the key sentences underlined
□ Provide a sentence starter: "I found the AI got ______ wrong; it should be ______, because ______."
□ They may mark only ✓ / ✗ / ? without writing a full sentence
□ Pair them with a stronger reader; the teacher explains once more at their desk
□ Provide pictures (a tram photo, a map) to aid understanding
More challenge (for pairs that finish early):
□ Write a better question to ask the AI
□ Find a second source and check again
□ Explain why the AI might be wrong (for example, it mixed up places, or its information is out of date)

4. Source cards (photocopy, one per pair)

Source card A (for Example 1) Hong Kong trams (the "ding ding") run only along the northern shore of Hong Kong Island, from Kennedy Town to Shau Kei Wan. They pass Central, Admiralty, Wan Chai, Causeway Bay, North Point and Shau Kei Wan. Sha Tin is in the New Territories and has no trams.
Source card B (for Example 2) Hong Kong's public transport includes the MTR, buses, minibuses, taxis, trams, harbour ferries and the Light Rail. Harbour ferries connect Hong Kong Island and Kowloon, for example Central to Tsim Sha Tsui. The Central to Cheung Chau service is an outlying-island ferry, not a harbour ferry.

5. Pupil worksheet

Name: ______________ Class: ______ Partner: ______________

Example 1 (whole class together)

The AI was asked: "Where do Hong Kong trams run? Which places do they pass?" The AI answered: "Hong Kong trams run mainly on Hong Kong Island. Places they pass include Central, Causeway Bay, and Sha Tin."

Check: Central ( ) Causeway Bay ( ) Sha Tin ( ) ← mark ✓ or ✗ or ?

Example 2 (in pairs)

The AI was asked: "Name three types of public transport in Hong Kong. Where are they used?" The AI answered: "Hong Kong has three main types of public transport: the MTR, buses, and the harbour ferry. The harbour ferry connects Hong Kong Island and Kowloon, for example Central to Cheung Chau."

1. In the AI's answer, circle the "claims" it makes (the facts it states).

2. Compare with source card B and mark each claim ✓ (right), ✗ (wrong) or ? (not sure).

3. Write one sentence: what is wrong, and how to fix it?

(Challenge) Why do you think the AI got it wrong?

6. Answer key and marking notes

RightWrongA good answer should include
Example 1 Central and Causeway Bay have trams. Sha Tin has no trams (it is in the New Territories). "What is wrong" plus "how to fix it": replace "Sha Tin" with a Hong Kong Island location (for example North Point).
Example 2 Harbour ferries connect Hong Kong Island and Kowloon. Central to Cheung Chau is not a harbour ferry, it is an outlying-island ferry. Point out that "Central to Cheung Chau" is not a harbour ferry, and change it to "Central to Tsim Sha Tsui".
Marking notes:
· Can they circle at least one claim the AI makes?
· Can they compare with the source card and mark ✓ / ✗ / ? correctly?
· Can they say "what is wrong and how to fix it"? Writing only "the AI is wrong" without saying where = not met; stating the error and proposing a fix = met.
· A good challenge answer: the specific error + a suggested fix + a guess at why the AI got it wrong.

7. Post-lesson reflection (teacher, three questions)

1. Which step was hardest for pupils when finding the AI's error? (circling the claims / comparing with the source / saying how to fix it)
2. Which pupil or pair needs support next time? How will I adjust?
3. Next time, in a different subject, which part will I keep and which part will I change?
