How to use this guide

  1. The three activities each take about 15-20 minutes and can be done on separate days.
  2. You need only pen and paper; no accounts or special equipment.
  3. After the three activities, sign the "Family homework agreement" together.
  4. A teacher or home-school coordinator can adapt the "Parent notice" into the school's own version before sending it home.

Activity 1 — Try it first (15-20 min)

  1. Pick a homework question the child has not yet done.
  2. The child tries it their own way (3-5 min), writing "what I tried" and "where I got stuck" separately in the box below.
  3. The parent asks only "What did you try?", and does not give the answer.

Activity 2 — Learn to ask for help (15-20 min)

  1. The parent plays the "AI helper"; the child plays the "learner asking for help".
  2. The child practises asking using the phrases below (read them first, then rephrase in their own words).
  3. The parent replies by explaining the steps only, not doing the work.
  4. Swap roles once, so the child can see what a good question looks like.
□ "Please explain the steps; do not give me the answer."
□ "Please point out where I went wrong; do not do it for me."
□ "I am stuck at ______. Could you explain that step?"

Talk about: why is asking for steps better than asking for the answer?

Activity 3 — Check the answer (15-20 min)

  1. The parent prepares a "wrong answer" in advance (use the fictional example below, or make one from the child's own homework).
  2. The child compares the answer with the question, finds the mistake and explains it in their own words.

Family homework agreement

Read each point aloud together, tick the box when everyone agrees, then fill in the school name, class and date, and sign.

□ Try it first — I will try the work myself before deciding whether to ask AI for help.
□ Help, not answers — when I ask AI, I will ask for the steps to be explained, not for the answer.
□ Check before I use — before using an AI answer, I will check whether it is correct and suitable.
□ Say when I used AI — when I use AI, I will tell my teacher or parent as the school rules require.
□ The parent's role — in early primary I will sit alongside, ask more than I answer, and do less for the child; in upper primary I will ask regularly "Did you use AI, and for which part?"
□ School rules come first — if the school has its own homework or AI-use rules, those rules take precedence.

Teacher notes: how to introduce this at a parents' evening

  1. Opening (2 min) — Say one line: "Children will use AI; parents need not worry, as long as the habits are set."
  2. Demonstrate "Activity 3 — Check the answer" (5 min) — Use the fictional example above to demonstrate finding the mistake on the spot.
  3. Hand out the guide (1 min) — Ask parents to start with Activity 1 at home, then do the rest over the following days.
  4. Reminder (2 min) — This guide is a helping tool and is not a substitute for the school's own homework and AI-use policy; where the school already has rules, those rules take precedence.

Answer to the fictional example: for "24 / 4 + 2", multiply and divide before adding and subtracting, so 24 / 4 = 6 first, then add 2; the correct answer is 8. The wrong answer added brackets that changed the order of operations.

The key idea: the difference between "help" and "doing it for them" is whether the child tries first and asks for an explanation rather than an answer. The habit in one line: try it first → ask for help → check before you use.
