One-term AI education plan

BEETS AI · Theme A · Resource #1 · v0.1 (draft) · School planning · P1-S3

⚠️ All examples on this page are fictional and do not refer to any real school or person. This file is a draft and must be reviewed by an educator before publication.

How to use

  1. Fill the blank planning table with this term's two pilot activities.
  2. For each activity, fill in the owner, dates and review evidence.
  3. Check that the plan is executable using the leader's checklist.
  4. When done, turn the page to compare with the fictional school's completed example.

Leader's checklist

□ Two pilot activities chosen, achievable within one term
□ Every activity has an owner (a named person or post)
□ Every activity has a date or week
□ Every activity states its review evidence — what to look at afterwards
□ Each activity can be tied to the EDB framework (B p.2 or F pp.1-6 to 1-10)
□ Mid-term and end-of-term review meetings have dates and an agenda
□ It is confirmed who collects and keeps the evidence

Completed example (fictional school)

The plan below is for a fictional primary school over one term (about 12 weeks), shown for illustration only. The owner names and dates are fictional.
Pilot activity Owner Dates / week Review evidence (what to look at afterwards)
1. Whole-school staff workshop: understand the AI literacy framework and the school's direction Ms Chan, curriculum coordinator Week 2 (90 min) Attendance record, post-session questionnaire, a first draft of the school's AI-use agreement
2. Cross-subject pilot lesson: one P5 General Studies period on "checking an AI answer" Mr Lee, General Studies panel head, with the P5 teachers Weeks 5-7 (one period per class) Three anonymised pupil worksheets + a panel review note
3. Mid-term and end-of-term review Ms Chan, curriculum coordinator Weeks 6 and 12 Evidence folder + a decision on next term's scope
The key judgement: the review-evidence column is what matters. If you cannot say "what we will look at afterwards", the activity cannot be reviewed, which means it has not really been planned.

How this relates to the EDB framework

Maps to B (the Digital Education Blueprint leaflet, p.2): digital and AI education strategies in school development plans with a clear implementation timetable; and F (the EDB AI literacy learning framework, pp.1-6 to 1-10): cross-subject implementation and progression across four learning stages.
