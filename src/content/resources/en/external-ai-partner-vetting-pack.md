> The hardest part of an outside workshop is never whether it was any good. It is three questions: why this provider, what did pupils hand over, and where did the money come from and how is it accounted for. Answer them before the event and there is a record afterwards.

## What this solves

Every year a school receives offers of AI talks, workshops and in-school courses from outside providers. The reason for saying yes is usually a single one: the topic fits, the date fits, and no teacher has to prepare. Six months later the questions are five: who found this provider, what exactly was said on the day, was any pupil data collected, how was the fee booked, and would we invite them again.

Four situations come up most often:

- **There is only a flyer.** The decision was made by a coordinator on the phone, and the school holds no sheet recording what was checked, who checked it, or what the conclusion was.
- **Content and data are vetted separately.** A subject head reads the content, an administrator reads the data, each sees half, and nobody asks whether the two halves still hold together.
- **The fee category and the funding source are never written down.** The problem surfaces at booking, when one item turns out not to be payable from that grant, and another budget is found at short notice.
- **The event ends when it ends.** With no after-event record, next year starts from zero and the same questions are asked again.

This page puts those four things into one document: **five one-page forms, three outcomes, four requirements on the day**.

**Three things to say plainly first:**

1. **The name and the five-form split on this page are a suggestion from this resource, not an Education Bureau requirement.** The Bureau's own wording for a school drawing on outside resources is "**utilising community resources**" and "**promoting cross-sector collaboration**"; this page only turns those two phrases into something that can be checked item by item and kept on file. A school may add or drop columns.
2. **This page is not an Education Bureau list of approved providers, and it is not an assessment of any organisation.** No provider is endorsed by filling in these five forms, and none is condemned by failing to; what is vetted is **this one arrangement**, not that organisation.
3. **Paying for an outside activity from the "Smart Learning with AI" grant must follow that scheme's guidelines and its accountability requirements; this page does not replace the funding report.** What may and may not be charged, what receipts are needed and what the report looks like are all settled by that scheme's guidelines, and this page sets up no second set.

## Purpose

By the end of this pack a school will be able to:

1. Use **five one-page forms** to carry one outside activity from "somebody suggested it" to "there is a record of it": partner details, content review, pupil data and accounts, fees and funding, after-event record — one page each, each photocopiable on its own.
2. Use **three outcomes** (usable as it stands / usable with conditions / not usable) to reach a conclusion before the event that can be written down and explained, instead of "it should be fine".
3. Use the **four requirements on the day** to carry the assumptions of the vetting into the room, and to say who may stop the session.
4. Keep fees expressed as a **category and a funding source** only, with amounts left in the quotation and procurement file, so the two are never confused.
5. Give incidents a fixed form of words: anything involving personal data or an account is "handled under the school's existing procedures and the requirements of the law", intellectual property is "per the terms of the contract", and the incident is recorded in the whole-school AI risk register.

The pack is built on the guiding principles of **nurturing the whole person, literacy first, application-oriented use and AI for good**, and around the three learning strands of AI literacy (the fundamental operating principles of AI, human-AI communication and collaboration, and reflection on the application of AI). It is a **vetting** document rather than a booking form because once an outside resource enters a classroom the school is responsible for those two lessons: the speaker can leave, the school stays. **Critical discernment**, **timely and appropriate use**, **human-AI collaboration** and **technology for good** look, with a visiting provider, most concretely like this: the school asks before agreeing which learning strand a segment serves, and writes down afterwards what to watch for next time.

## How to use it

The file is a Word document in six parts: the five facts at the top, the five one-page forms (partner details, content review, pupil data and accounts, fees and funding, after-event record), the three outcomes, the four requirements on the day (one page, for laminating and taking to the room), the boundary with five of the school's own documents, and a one-page proofreading list. The curriculum lead should own the vetting; the data and fees forms are filled in by the data-protection colleague and the administrator, and the same curriculum lead puts the five together into one outcome.

### Step 1: five one-page forms, and only five

The five are fixed; none is added and none removed. Each is one page, each has one person filling it in, and each says when it is due.

| Form | What the page holds | Who fills it in | Due |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Partner details | Provider category, type of service, contact post, activity name, date, year groups, numbers, venue, previous activities of the same kind (category and year only) | The coordinating colleague | Fourteen days before |
| 2 Content review | Which learning strand each segment serves, learning objectives, whether examples are accurate and age-appropriate, any commercial promotion, length and running order, any follow-up activity | The subject head or curriculum lead | Fourteen days before |
| 3 Pupil data and accounts | Whether pupil data is collected, whether accounts are used, photographs and recording, ownership of pupils' work, what happens to the data afterwards | The data-protection or administrative colleague | Seven days before |
| 4 Fees and funding | Fee categories, funding source, prior approval, procurement procedure, what that grant may and may not pay for | The administrative colleague | Seven days before |
| 5 After-event record | Date, year groups, approximate attendance, the outcome reached, anything changed on the day, one sentence on incidents, what to watch for next time | The colleague who was present | Within a week after |

Three things worth saying about using them:

- **Form 1 carries categories, never names.** The provider's name stays in the school's own file, not on a pack that gets photocopied and carried into meetings; that is what stops a vetting form turning into a provider list.
- **Form 2 has to reach "which learning strand each segment serves".** A segment that cannot be mapped is the first condition of "usable with conditions": cut it, or have the mapping written.
- **Form 5 is completed within a week.** A month later the only thing anyone remembers is that "the reaction was quite good", and "what changed on the day" is precisely what the next vetting needs.

### Step 2: three outcomes, and only three

The outcome goes on the last line of form 2 and into what is said to school management; no separate memo is written.

| Outcome | What it means | What has to be written |
|---|---|---|
| Usable as it stands | Both the content and the data forms pass, with no conditions attached | The date and the post of the person deciding |
| Usable with conditions | It can go ahead once conditions that can be written down are met | Each condition as one checkable action, the post that checks it, and when the check is done |
| Not usable | The data form fails, or the core of the content does not match the learning objectives | One sentence of reason, and a note that the person who proposed it has been told |

Three boundaries, none optional:

- **"Usable with conditions" may not say "handle it carefully".** "Carefully" is not a condition; "cut the live account-registration segment from part 3, and the coordinating colleague checks the speaker's slides against the approved version the day before" is.
- **The data form sets the floor.** However good the content, if pupil data would be collected and the school has not arranged for it under its existing procedures, the outcome is not usable. Conversely, ordinary content with clean data handling can be usable as it stands.
- **The outcome is about one event, not about the organisation.** The same provider with a different topic or a different speaker is vetted again; last time's outcome does not carry over.

### Step 3: the four requirements on the day

The four are fixed, printed on one page, laminated and taken to the room. None of them is a suggestion.

| No. | Requirement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A school colleague is present throughout**, with the authority to stop any segment there and then | The vetting read the slides; the speaker speaks. The colleague in the room is the only person who can compare the two |
| 2 | **No pupil personal data is collected on the day, and pupils are not asked to register an account or install an application on the spot**; where a demonstration is needed, use a school account or school-provided sample data | Registering on the spot means opening a batch of new accounts in a classroom that nobody clears up afterwards |
| 3 | **The content matches the version that was vetted**; where something has to change on the day, the colleague present is told first and it goes into the after-event record | A change nobody records means the next vetting starts from a version that never existed |
| 4 | **Photographs, recording and pupils' work are handled under the school's existing procedures and the requirements of the law**; intellectual property is per the terms of the contract | On the day these two are the easiest things to treat as incidental, and neither is the speaker's to decide |

Form 5 is completed within a week of the event. Anything involving personal data or an account goes into the whole-school AI risk register, with no second form opened.

### Step 4: fees and funding, category and source only

Form 4 holds two things: the **fee category** and the **funding source**.

- **Fees are written as a category only**: speaker fee, materials, transport, venue, equipment, sundries. **Neither this page nor the file states any amount**; amounts belong in the quotation, procurement and booking documents, and the pack only says which category an item falls into.
- **The funding source is written as a name only**: for example the "Smart Learning with AI" grant, the school development grant, the parent-teacher association, or other (fill in). One activity paid from two sources gets two lines.
- **Payment from the "Smart Learning with AI" grant must follow that scheme's guidelines and its accountability requirements.** What may and may not be charged, and what receipts and reports are required, follow that scheme's guidelines; **this page does not replace the funding report**, and it does not replace the school's procurement and finance procedures.
- **Never write "do it now, approve it later".** Not starting until prior approval is complete is the most practical line on this form; an approval signed afterwards is no help when the report is written.

### Step 5: how the content review maps to the three learning strands

The middle of form 2 asks for each segment of the activity to be mapped to one of the three learning strands of AI literacy:

1. **The fundamental operating principles of AI** — does the segment explain how the tool arrives at an answer, and where its limits are.
2. **Human-AI communication and collaboration** — does the segment let pupils write their own instructions and judge for themselves which part to change.
3. **Reflection on the application of AI** — does the segment leave time to ask whether this use is appropriate, and whether anyone is affected by it.

All three are not required: a two-lesson talk that lands one strand properly is doing well. What has to be written is **which segment serves which strand**, and **which strand is not covered at all** — the second becomes a condition of "usable with conditions", and the usual answer is a follow-up lesson run by a school teacher.

### Step 6: the boundary with five of the school's own documents

Each does its own part; none replaces another:

- **The school AI-use policy template**: the policy layer of what is and is not allowed; this page is the layer that says how one outside activity actually works.
- **The staff data-handling pack**: the list of what may and may not be put into a tool; quoted here, not repeated.
- **The whole-school AI risk register and incident process**: incidents go into the same one; this page opens no second form.
- **The AI tool evaluation and procurement rubric**: it scores tools the school adopts, not outside providers; this page scores no organisation and changes none of that rubric's items.
- **The parent communication pack**: a provider coming into school, and the arrangements for photographs and recording, are explained there; this page handles the vetting only and writes no separate letter home.

### Step 7: four things that must not appear (proofreading list)

This section is for the person filling in the forms and the person checking them; tick each line when the vetting is complete.

1. **No named organisation and no commercial brand anywhere.** Categories only; the provider's name stays in the school's own file.
2. **No price anywhere.** Fees are a category and a funding source; amounts live in the quotation and procurement documents.
3. **No claim that an outside talk counts automatically towards a teacher's 30 training hours.** Whether it counts depends on the relevant circulars and the scheme's guidelines, and the school checks it; this page makes no such claim and promises nothing on the school's behalf.
4. **Personal data is always written as "handled under the school's existing procedures and the requirements of the law"; intellectual property as "per the terms of the contract".** No number of days, deadline, body name or clause text is written here — written here it goes out of date with the next amendment, and it reads as if this page were the requirement.

### Step 8: primary and secondary

The three learning strands and the five-form split are the same in both sectors. The difference is **where the evidence comes from**: the AI component of the Primary Information and Innovation Technology curriculum framework is a **primary-stage trial version**, so a primary school can put that framework's topics straight into the "learning content it maps to" column of form 2. **At secondary level there is no equivalent official framework**, so a secondary school rests only on the AI Literacy Learning Framework, the Guide to Using AI in Teaching and the Education Bureau circulars, and must not write it as an Education Bureau requirement. A trial version is not final, and a school should adjust it to its own situation.

## Review checklist

Once the vetting is complete and the event has happened, check each line:

- □ All five forms are present, in the order above, with no sixth added.
- □ Every form has a person filling it in and a due date; none is left blank.
- □ Form 1 carries provider categories only, and no named organisation or commercial brand appears anywhere.
- □ Form 2 maps each segment to a learning strand, and says which strand is not covered at all.
- □ Form 3 says whether pupil data is collected, whether accounts are used, and how photographs and recording are handled.
- □ Form 4 carries fee categories and funding sources only, and no amount appears anywhere.
- □ The page says payment from the "Smart Learning with AI" grant follows that scheme's guidelines and its accountability requirements, and that this page does not replace the funding report.
- □ Exactly one of the three outcomes appears, with its date and the post of the person deciding.
- □ Where the outcome is "usable with conditions", every condition is a checkable action with a post and a date.
- □ The page says the outcome is about one event, not an assessment of the organisation.
- □ The four requirements on the day are printed and were taken to the room.
- □ The colleague present is recorded as having the authority to stop the session.
- □ The after-event record was completed within a week, and says whether anything changed on the day.
- □ Anything involving personal data or an account is written as "handled under the school's existing procedures and the requirements of the law" and recorded in the whole-school AI risk register.
- □ Intellectual property is written as "per the terms of the contract", with no clause text quoted.
- □ Nothing anywhere claims that an outside talk counts automatically towards a teacher's 30 training hours.
- □ The page says the name and the five-form split are this resource's suggestion, not an Education Bureau requirement, and gives the Bureau's own wording, "utilising community resources" and "promoting cross-sector collaboration".
- □ The page says it is not an Education Bureau list of approved providers and is not an assessment of any organisation.
- □ The page says the AI component of the primary framework is a trial version and that there is no equivalent official framework at secondary level.
- □ Contacts and duties are always written as posts, and no person's name appears.

## Official documents

Every resource in this library is written to the AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools and falls within the policy scope of the Blueprint for Digital Education Development in Primary and Secondary Schools.

This resource maps to:

- **Blueprint for Digital Education Development in Primary and Secondary Schools** (three-fold leaflet, p.2): its four principles are technology for good, nurturing the whole person, equity and access, and forward-looking innovation; this page places "inviting an outside partner" under nurturing the whole person and equity and access — every class should get an arrangement of the same quality, rather than whichever teacher happens to know a speaker.
- **Supplement I: AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools** (p.1-4): sets out the three learning strands; the content review in step 2 requires each segment of the activity to be mapped to them, and a segment that cannot be mapped is conditional or not usable.
- **Supplement II: Guide to Using AI in Teaching in Primary and Secondary Schools** (p.2-4): the teacher's professional gatekeeping, compliance with the law, and safe learning; the five forms and the four requirements on the day are that section, applied to an outside activity. (pp.2-2 to 2-5) The seven principles open with nurturing the whole person, with technology as an aid; the division of responsibility on this page follows: the vetting is done by school colleagues, and a speaker does not decide for the school.
- **EDB Circular No. 156/2026** (Appendix 1, section 3): upholding professional gatekeeping; "a school colleague is present throughout, with authority to stop the session" is how that section lands. (Appendix 1, section 4) attending to pupils' physical and mental well-being and cultivating habits of appropriate use of digital tools; the requirement not to register accounts or install applications on the spot is written to it.
- **EDB Circular No. 107/2026** (paragraph 4): bringing digital education into the school development plan and/or the annual school plan, and reviewing it through a self-evaluation cycle; the after-event record on this page is one piece of evidence for that cycle, in the context of an outside activity.
