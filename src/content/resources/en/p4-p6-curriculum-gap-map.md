## What this solves

Many schools already teach AI-literacy-related content in different subjects — General Studies teaches "checking a source", ICT teaches "not giving away personal data", Chinese Language teaches "telling true from false information" — but these scattered activities sit in different subjects, and no one knows which goals are already covered, which are repeated, and which are not taught at all.

This workbook turns that step into a single curriculum meeting of about 60 minutes: first record each subject's existing AI-literacy activities in a "subject × term" table, then check them against the P4-P6 stage goals, mark the gaps, and write the smallest next step for each gap. Once the table is filled in, a panel head can see where P4-P6 AI literacy already fits across the subjects, and where it is still empty.

## Learning objectives

After the curriculum meeting, principals, curriculum coordinators and panel heads can:

1. Use the "subject × term" table to record the AI-literacy elements that already appear in the school's existing teaching.
2. Check them against the P4-P6 stage goals and judge which goals are covered, which are repeated, and which are empty.
3. Write a "next step" for each gap (one small lesson or activity change) and assign an owner.
4. Use the three completed fictional school examples to judge whether a coverage map "leads to a next step" rather than just listing activities.

## Teaching steps

This is a curriculum-meeting agenda of about 60 minutes (it can be folded into a panel or administrative meeting):

1. **Opening and shared aim (5 min)** — State that the goal is not "full rollout" but seeing where P4-P6 AI literacy already fits across the subjects.
2. **Explain the stage goals (10 min)** — Project the P4-P6 stage-goal list and ask each panel head to tick off, in their head, which goals their subject already teaches.
3. **Fill in the coverage table (20 min)** — Subject by subject, record existing activities in the "subject × term" table: existing first, then empty.
4. **Mark the gaps (15 min)** — Against the goal list, mark the "not covered" and "repeated" positions.
5. **Write the next steps (10 min)** — For each gap, write one small next step and assign an owner; leave blanks to be finished after the meeting.

## Answer key and marking notes

There is no single right answer, but a coverage map that "leads to a next step" can be judged against the checklist below.

**P4-P6 stage goals (suggested list)** [[VERIFY]]: the goal rows below are a working scaffold and must be checked word for word against the P4-P6 stage goals in F (the AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools, pp.1-6 to 1-10) before they are quoted.

| Goal | Suggested P4-P6 goal (check against F pp.1-6 to 1-10) |
|---|---|
| G1 | Understand what AI is and where it appears in everyday life |
| G2 | Use AI safely and responsibly, and know what information should not be entered or shared |
| G3 | Check AI's output against other sources and spot what is questionable |
| G4 | Understand AI's limits and bias, and know that data affects results |
| G5 | Use AI to support creating and collaborating under teacher guidance, keeping one's own judgement |
| G6 | Understand data and personal privacy, and know how information is collected and used |

**Coverage-judgement checklist**:

| Check item | Leads to a next step | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Existing activities | States "which subject, which level, what was taught" | Only "we teach AI", no specifics |
| Against the goals | Every goal is marked "covered / repeated / empty" | Only a list of activities, no check |
| Gaps | Every gap has one smallest next step | Only the word "strengthen" |
| Owner | Every next step has a name or post | Only "the panel" |
| Next step | One change achievable in a term | Too large, needs separate planning |

> The key judgement: **the "next step" column is what matters.** If every gap only gets "teach more AI", the map is not useful; each next step must be small enough to finish within one term.
