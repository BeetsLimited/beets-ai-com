## What this solves

When students use AI to complete their work, teachers often see only the finished product — they cannot tell what the student thought for themselves, what the AI contributed, or whether the student checked anything. Asking students to hand over the entire conversation log is neither practical nor good for privacy.

This process sheet fixes the process in place with **five boxes** — manageable for students to fill in, and visible to teachers.

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

1. Attempt the task themselves before using AI (keeping their own initial thinking).
2. Record the prompt they used and explain why they asked it that way.
3. Check the AI's output and point out at least one place that needs verifying or changing.
4. Explain how the final version differs from the first attempt, and why.

## Teaching steps

1. **Briefing (5 min)** — Project the process sheet and explain what to write in each of the five boxes; stress that the "first attempt" must be written before opening the AI.
2. **Demonstration (5 min)** — Use one question to demonstrate how to check an AI's answer (for example, by comparing what the AI says against the textbook / an official source).
3. **Task (students complete independently)** — Students fill in the sheet as they work.
4. **Collection and feedback** — The teacher can judge the learning process from the process sheet alone, without reading through the conversation.
5. **Optional extension** — Pick three process sheets (anonymised) and discuss as a class: whose checking is the most convincing?

## The five boxes

| Box | What the student writes | Why this box |
|---|---|---|
| 1 First attempt | My own idea / answer before using the AI | Keeps the student's original thinking so it isn't replaced by the AI |
| 2 My prompt | What I asked (verbatim) | Lets the teacher see the quality of the questioning, and lets the student reflect |
| 3 The AI's answer | What the AI said (a summary is enough) | Separates "what the student wrote" from "what the AI gave" |
| 4 What I checked | Which part I verified / changed, and what source I used | This is the most valuable evidence of learning |
| 5 Final version and explanation | The final version + one sentence on "why I changed it this way" | The student summarises in their own words |

> Box 4 is the key to the whole sheet. If a student cannot complete box 4, it means they did not check, and the teacher can follow up immediately.

## Notes for teachers

- **Complete box 1 first.** If a student opens the AI before writing, the sheet loses its point; it is worth completing box 1 together in class first.
- **Box 4 does not need to be long.** One sentence — "I checked textbook p.42, and the AI got the year wrong" — is already enough.
- **Do not ask for the full conversation log.** The whole point of the process sheet is to replace that practice, and to reduce the collection of student data.
- **Works for any subject.** The sheet is not tied to a particular subject — mathematics, general studies, and languages all work.

## How this relates to the EDB framework

This resource maps to:

- **F (the AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools, p.1-13)**: values process and authentic assessment rather than judging only the final result.
- **G (the Guide to Using AI in Teaching in Primary and Secondary Schools, p.2-8)**: recommends checking students' learning and AI use through oral questioning and the submission of their thinking process.
