## What this solves

Digital education and AI literacy are meant to be delivered **across subjects**, and in practice each panel assumes another panel is doing it. The result is that some content is taught three times over and some learning strands never appear at all between P1 and S6 — while the school holds only a list of activities, with no way to see where the gaps are.

This workbook turns coverage into a grid you can look at: **eight learning areas broken down into thirty subjects, by three learning strands, by four learning stages**. Each cell does not record "whether we teach AI"; it records whether this subject has covered this strand — taught, partial, or not taught.

**Breaking the grid down to subject level is what makes the tool usable.** With only the eight learning areas, a Chinese Language teacher, a Music teacher and a PE teacher all fail to find their own subject on the sheet, and the school is back to each panel assuming another one has it. Listed subject by subject, every teacher can go straight to their own row and see which strand their subject still leaves blank.

The resource falls within the policy scope of the Blueprint for Digital Education Development in Primary and Secondary Schools and is written to the AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools: the Blueprint asks a school to plan how digital and AI education will be delivered in its School Development Plan, and EDBCM No. 156/2026 asks schools to build digital education into the School Development Plan and/or the Annual School Plan. The coverage audit is what turns that requirement into evidence — which subject, which stage, which strand — and the gap list also names the tiered and learning-data arrangements and who carries the professional gatekeeping.

> Use the current subject names throughout: **Primary Humanities** and **Primary Science** (they replace General Studies; all primary schools have been required to implement them from the 2025/26 school year — see EDBCM No. 20/2023 and No. 18/2023). Update the subject names in the audit grid accordingly.

## Planning objectives

After one curriculum meeting, principals, curriculum coordinators and panel heads can:

1. Use the grid — eight learning areas, thirty subjects, three strands, four stages — to record which strands the school **already** covers.
2. Name the subjects, strands or stages with **no coverage at all**, and the places where several subjects duplicate the same coverage.
3. Write one smallest next step for each gap, with the panel responsible and an approximate date.
4. Use a partly completed example from a fictional school to judge whether an audit "leads to a next step" rather than being a marked-up table.

The audit keeps nurturing the whole person, literacy first, application-oriented use and technology for good in view; next steps are designed with learning diversity and tiered arrangements in mind, and professional gatekeeping is exercised where it belongs — in the classroom.

## Planning steps

One 60-minute curriculum meeting, in four segments.

1. **State the purpose and claim the learning areas (15 min)** — This is not an appraisal of teachers; it is to find out where the three strands still have no landing place, in which subjects and at which stages. Whoever is present speaks for the learning areas represented; for any area with nobody in the room, the curriculum coordinator fills it in afterwards rather than leaving it empty and reading it as "none".
2. **Fill in the grid (20 min)** — In the subject coverage sheet, record for each subject a short name of the existing activity with its year level and term, and mark each of the three strands taught, partial or not taught. Primary teachers can filter on the applicable-stages column to see junior primary and upper primary only, without wading through secondary subjects.
3. **Read the gaps (18 min)** — The stage-by-strand summary sheet shows automatically how many subjects at each stage have marked a strand as taught; a 0 means no coverage at all. Then list the subjects or stages with no landing place in the gaps sheet, and flag the cells covered by three or more subjects.
4. **Write the next steps (7 min)** — For each gap, write one smallest next step, sorted into short term (one lesson or one co-planning session this term), medium term (plan it next term) and long term (it has to be handled at curriculum-document level), with the panel responsible.

**The dimensions of the grid**

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Learning areas (eight) | Chinese Language Education · English Language Education · Mathematics Education · Science Education · Technology Education · Personal, Social and Humanities Education · Arts Education · Physical Education |
| Subjects (thirty) | listed below; the workbook gives each one a row and keeps a final blank row for a school to add its own subject, an Applied Learning course, or another language subject |
| Learning strands (three) | the fundamental operating principles of AI · human-AI communication and collaboration · reflection on the application of AI |
| Learning stages (four) | Junior primary · Upper primary · Junior secondary · Senior secondary |
| What a cell records | taught / partial / not taught, plus a column for the unit or topic already taught and a notes column |

## Review checklist

There is no single right answer, but an audit that "leads to a next step" can be checked against these points:

| Check item | Leads to a next step | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage of the grid | all thirty subjects filled in, including the panels with nobody in the room (decided afterwards) | only the eight or nine panels that had someone present |
| Level of detail | filled in subject by subject, each subject with its own landing places and gaps | filled in only to learning-area level, with the subjects left blank |
| Basis of the entry | each cell says which subject, which year level, which activity | "we teach AI" |
| Gaps | every blank cell has one smallest next step, sorted into short, medium and long term | "we need better cross-subject coordination" |
| Duplication | cells covered by several subjects are flagged, and the school decides which to keep | duplication ignored, new activities added on top |
| Owners and dates | each next step has a panel and an approximate date | "each panel to follow up" |

> The key judgement: **the next-step column is what matters.** If every gap only gets "teach more AI", the audit is not useful; each next step must be small enough to finish within one term. Note also that a gap does not automatically mean new curriculum time — approaching an existing activity from a different angle is often enough.

> The activity names in the audit are the school's own internal record and need not be published; the workbook also recommends naming no commercial AI tool.

## Official documents

Every resource in this library is written to the AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools and falls within the policy scope of the Blueprint for Digital Education Development in Primary and Secondary Schools.

This resource maps to:

- **Supplement I: AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools** (pp.1-4 and 1-6 to 1-10): sets out the three learning strands and the progression across four learning stages, and describes how AI education is to be taken forward across the learning areas and cross-subject contexts — the source of the audit grid's three dimensions.
- **Blueprint for Digital Education Development in Primary and Secondary Schools** (three-fold leaflet, p.2): requires digital and AI education strategies in school development plans, with a clear implementation timetable.
- **EDBCM No. 156/2026** (2026/27 School Year Holistic Planning of School-based Digital Education and Related Teacher Training): states that schools must build digital education into the School Development Plan and/or the Annual School Plan in the 2026/27 school year; this resource is the audit that turns that requirement into cross-subject coverage evidence.
- **EDBCM No. 20/2023 and No. 18/2023**: set out the implementation arrangements for Primary Humanities and Primary Science (which replace General Studies), and the current subject names used in the audit grid.
- **The curriculum guides and curriculum documents of each learning area**: the source of the thirty subject names and their learning areas in the grid.
