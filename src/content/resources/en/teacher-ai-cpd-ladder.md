> Thirty hours is not a number, it is an arrangement: filled in over three years, across rungs, across course categories. What a school actually has to produce is not a stack of certificates but the answer to "who has done how much, and in which category".

## What this solves

The official requirement is fairly specific. Within each three-year continuing professional development cycle (150 hours), a teacher must complete **no fewer than 30 hours** of digital education training. At least **50,000** training places are provided each year. The training is arranged as **9 units** of **6 hours** each, at two levels — "foundation application" and "advanced level" — and in three categories: AI literacy, AI plus subject, and AI leadership.

Knowing those figures is not hard. What is hard is three questions a school must answer every year:

- **Who has completed how many hours, and in which category?** An attendance sheet cannot answer this. Nor can most teachers say how far they are from 30 hours, or which category is missing.
- **How should the 30 hours be divided?** Putting the whole staff on the same course looks fair, but it makes a colleague who could already lead a subject panel sit through the basics again, and it drops a colleague who has never used AI straight into the advanced level.
- **"Trained" and "the classroom changed" are two different things.** Hours can be filled in. Without a corresponding classroom behaviour, the school can only say "we ran training" — which is not the question it set itself.

This resource puts five things on one page: the official figures, a **four-rung ladder with suggested hours**, a **table of the three official course categories**, a **school-based training record**, and **how to enrol**. The four tables depend on each other — without the ladder the record is just an attendance list, and without the record the ladder is just a statement.

## Purpose

After one planning meeting of about 60 minutes, a school can:

1. Read out the five official figures, and use the same wording in its own documents — development plan, annual plan, training plan.
2. Place each teacher on one of the four rungs and record that rung's suggested cumulative hours as the basis for this year's course choices.
3. Choose courses from the three-category table, so that every hour maps to an official category and an official level rather than to "a course was available".
4. Answer "who has done how much, and what is missing" from one record, and close the gaps before the cycle ends.
5. Tell teachers to enrol with the **HKEdCity school-based teacher account**, instead of relying on a colleague forwarding a registration link.

> **An important boundary**: the four rungs, the suggested hours for each rung (6 / 12 / 18 / 24) and the split of "24 hours plus 6 school-based hours" are **this resource's suggestion, not an Education Bureau rule**. The rule is no fewer than 30 hours of digital education training within each three-year cycle; the ladder only turns that into four executable steps. A school may rename the rungs or change the hours, but the change should be written into the plan and fixed at the start of the cycle.

## How to use it

### Step 1: Read the official requirement first (about 10 minutes)

Do not design the ladder first; confirm the five figures. The most common error in school documents is to write "150 hours" and "30 hours" as two separate requirements, which reads as though 180 hours were needed.

**Table 1: the five official figures, and what each means for planning**

| Official requirement | Figure | What it means for planning |
|---|---|---|
| Continuing professional development per three-year cycle | 150 hours | The 30 hours sit inside this cycle; they are not added on top of it |
| Of which digital education training | No fewer than 30 hours | A per-teacher figure that the school record has to be able to show |
| Training places each year | No fewer than 50,000 | Supply is not the constraint; choosing the right course is |
| Training units | 9 units | Units have official names; copy the name into the record, do not invent one |
| Hours per unit | 6 hours | One unit is one countable item; hours accumulate by unit |

Two things are not numbers but still have to be written as the official documents write them: the two levels are **"foundation application"** and **"advanced level"**, and the three course categories are **AI literacy**, **AI plus subject** and **AI leadership**.

### Step 2: Place teachers on the ladder (about 20 minutes)

The ladder answers one question: **what does this colleague most need next?** It describes where someone is now, not a promotion every year — a head of department with ten years' teaching and no AI experience starts at rung one, and that is normal.

**Table 2: the four rungs and their suggested hours (this resource's suggestion)**

| Rung | Name | Suggested cumulative hours | Official level and category | What becomes visible in the classroom or panel afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First use | 6 hours | "Foundation application" · AI literacy | Can run one purposeful classroom demonstration on the school's approved account; can say which data must not be entered; knows the school's three rules of use |
| 2 | Classroom use | 12 hours | "Foundation application" · AI literacy and AI plus subject | Can design and teach one AI-supported learning activity in a unit they teach, and say which learning strand it serves |
| 3 | Subject-panel lead | 18 hours | "Advanced level" · AI plus subject | Can run joint planning with subject colleagues, review unit designs, and handle data and safety questions when pupils use AI |
| 4 | Curriculum lead | 24 hours | "Advanced level" · AI leadership | Can plan the school-wide spread across the three categories, account for hours and staffing to the school, and bring new colleagues in |

The four rungs total 24 hours. **The remaining 6 hours are allocated by the school** — a school-based project tied to one subject, for instance, or one other piece of digital education training the school judges most needed — making 30. There is one test of whether those 6 hours are well used: they must relate to a rung's capability, not simply top up a number.

Three ways to use the ladder:

1. **For individuals**: ask each teacher to self-assess a rung before the meeting, and discuss only the disagreements in the meeting — no roll-call of individuals.
2. **For panels**: plot the rung spread within each subject. A panel where most people sit at rung one needs a completely different course strategy from one where most are already at rung three.
3. **For the school**: the number of people at rung four is the school's own capacity to run training instead of buying all of it.

### Step 3: Choose courses from the three-category table (about 15 minutes)

The three categories are not a difficulty order; they are **types of problem**. Choose the wrong category and the hours still add up, while the school's actual problem stays unsolved.

**Table 3: the three official course categories**

| Official category | For | Rungs | Check before enrolling |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI literacy | All teachers | 1 and 2 | It is "foundation application"; the unit's official name is copied exactly; there is something you can take straight back to a classroom |
| AI plus subject | All teachers, by subject taught | 2 and 3 | It uses your own subject as the setting; there is a unit design and an assessment approach you can take away |
| AI leadership | Panel heads and school leadership | 4 | It is "advanced level"; it deals with school-wide planning, resources and accountability |

The order that works is: **decide the problem this rung has to solve, then find the category, then check the level.** Doing it the other way round usually means seeing a course being promoted and then finding a reason for it.

Two things to watch:

- **Use the official names.** That includes AI literacy, AI plus subject and AI leadership, and the two levels "foundation application" and "advanced level". Training the school runs itself — a staff sharing session, joint panel planning — may be recorded, but it must sit in a separate row from any official unit, and be marked as corresponding to no official unit.
- **One category can appear more than once.** Three AI plus subject units in three years is reasonable for one teacher. Three AI literacy units for the colleague who has been asked to lead a panel is not.

### Step 4: Fill in the school-based record (once a term, about 20 minutes)

The record is in the attachment, one row per teacher. The columns are: teacher, current rung, units completed (official names), category, level, hours, date completed, next step, notes.

Three rules for filling it in:

1. **One row per unit.** Do not compress three units into "AI training, 18 hours" — that cannot answer which category is missing.
2. **Count the unit's official hours**, not an estimate from attendance time.
3. **Write "next step" at the start of the term, not the end.** It is a plan, not a review.

> **The record's boundary belongs in its header**: this table is for school-based training planning and hour counting only. It is **not a staff appraisal instrument, and must not be used as grounds for refusing a teacher's application to attend further training**. A table with levels and hours invites being read as a score sheet; putting the sentence in the header means the next person to use it does not have to guess.

### Step 5: Enrol (immediately)

Teachers enrol on the official courses with the **HKEdCity school-based teacher account**. If an account has not been activated, apply to the colleague who administers accounts for the school; that is better handled once at the start of the term than discovered on the day a course begins.

Two checks before enrolling: first, whether the course's category and level match the rung; second, write the unit name into the "next step" column, and fill in the date and hours only once it is completed.

## Review checklist

| Check | Done properly | Needs work |
|---|---|---|
| Official figures | The school's documents use one set of figures: 150 / 30 / 50,000 / 9 / 6 | The documents differ, or 30 hours is written as an extra requirement |
| Ladder | Every teacher has a rung, with the basis recorded | Only "trained / not trained" |
| Course categories | Every hour maps to one of the three categories and one official level | "AI training, 6 hours" and nothing more |
| Record | One row per unit, official names | Several units in one cell, or invented names in place of official units |
| Boundary | The header states the record is not an appraisal instrument | Used for appraisal, or to decide who may attend training |
| Enrolment | Teachers know to use the HKEdCity school-based teacher account | Relying on a forwarded link |
| The 6 hours | The school-based use is stated and tied to a rung | Left blank, or used only to reach 30 |

> The test: **the ladder is working when the school can say who is short of how many hours, and in which category.** "We ran six sessions this year" still leaves its own question unanswered.

> The ladder and its hours are this resource's suggestion. A school adopting them should say so in the same document, so that the arrangement is not later quoted back as an official requirement.

## Official documents

Every resource in this library is written to the AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools and falls within the policy scope of the Blueprint for Digital Education Development in Primary and Secondary Schools.

This resource maps to:

- **Blueprint for Digital Education Development in Primary and Secondary Schools** (three-fold leaflet, p.2): sets the teacher professional-development markers — no fewer than 30 hours of digital education training within each three-year continuing professional development cycle, no fewer than 50,000 training places a year, and leadership training handled separately from whole-staff training.
- **Supplement I: AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools** (pp.1-6 to 1-10): sets out progression across the learning stages in the three learning strands; the "what becomes visible" column on the ladder follows those strands rather than tool-handling fluency.
- **Supplement II: Guide to Using AI in Teaching in Primary and Secondary Schools** (pp.2-2 to 2-5): sets out what is required when AI is used in teaching, including teachers' professional gatekeeping, where the assessment decision sits, and how learning data is handled; the capability descriptions at rungs three and four follow it.
- **EDBCM No. 156/2026** (2026/27 School Year Holistic Planning of School-based Digital Education and Related Teacher Training): lists the training units and the level tiers, and requires digital education to be built into the School Development Plan and/or the Annual School Plan.
- **EDBCM No. 107/2026** (Training on Implementing Artificial Intelligence Education Planning in Schools and Teacher Professional Training in Artificial Intelligence): sets out the training arrangements for a school implementing its AI education plan; the four steps here — plan, choose, record, review — follow that structure.

**Learning strand**: fundamental operating principles of AI / human-AI communication and collaboration / reflection on the application of AI
