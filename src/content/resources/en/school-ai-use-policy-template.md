> School-policy focus: **a school has to be able to write a rule and point back to the official document it rests on.**

## What this solves
In the 2026/27 school year, schools must build digital education into the School Development Plan and/or the Annual School Plan, and keep reviewing it through a plan–implement–evaluate self-evaluation cycle. The school's AI-use policy is the page of that plan that is asked about most often — and the hardest to draft: written too loosely, staff do not know what to do; written too tightly, it is out of date within a year.

This template breaks a school AI-use policy into eleven sections, each one anchored to a document the Education Bureau has already published, and adds a staff edition, a student edition and a completed example. The school fills in only its name, date, version, owner and review date.

## Purpose
By the end of this template a school will be able to:

1. Complete an AI-use policy it can release to staff, students and parents within one term.
2. State, for every section, the official document it rests on.
3. Express the staff and the student codes in one document, so the two cannot contradict each other.
4. Set a review cycle and an owner, so the policy is updated year by year instead of written once and left.

## How to use it
The file is one Word document in three parts: the **staff edition**, the **student edition** and a **completed example**.

**Part 1 — staff edition (eleven sections)**

1. Purpose and scope
2. School-level rollout, accountability, and the 2026/27 School Development Plan requirement
3. Staff rules (learning, teaching and assessment principles, and learning-data handling)
4. Student rules
5. Personal data and privacy
6. Academic integrity and assessment
7. Intellectual property and citation
8. Tool selection and approval
9. Incident handling
10. Parent communication and consent
11. Review cycle

**Part 2 — student edition**

The same rules rewritten as short sentences a pupil can read, for a form-teacher period or a student handbook; subject examples can be added to the "Academic integrity and assessment" and "Intellectual property and citation" sections.

**Part 3 — completed example**

A fictional school's filled-in version, there only to show how specific each field should be. Do not copy the school or personal names.

> Throughout the document, "the AI tools approved by the school" stands in for any product name. A policy is usually drafted before a tool is chosen; write the rules first and the tool name later, and the policy will not need rewriting every time the tool changes.

### Suggested order of work
1. **Fill in sections 1, 2 and 8 first** (scope, rollout and accountability, tool approval) — these three decide how the rest is written.
2. **Take sections 3, 6, 7 and 9 to a whole-staff meeting** and record the questions raised as the starting point of the next review.
3. **Show sections 4 and 10 to form teachers and parent representatives** to confirm that students and parents can both read them.
4. **Section 5** (personal data and privacy) is best checked alongside the school's existing personal information collection statement.
5. **Write section 11 into the Annual School Plan**, naming the next review date and its owner.

## Review checklist
Before the policy is released, check each item:

- Every section can state the official document it rests on.
- No commercial AI product is named anywhere in the document.
- The staff edition and the student edition say the same thing about the same question (for example, whether a student may use AI for a first draft).
- Every requirement names who is responsible for it.
- Section 11 has a clear review date and an owner.
- Any clause touching student personal data does not conflict with the school's existing personal information collection statement.
- The student edition contains no word a teacher would have to explain.

## Official documents

This resource maps to:

- **Blueprint for Digital Education Development in Primary and Secondary Schools** (leaflet, p.2): requires digital and AI education strategies to be built into the School Development Plan, with a clear implementation timetable.
- **Supplement I: AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools** (pp.1-6 to 1-10): sets out cross-subject implementation and the four learning-stage progressions, for a school planning the teaching and learning of AI literacy.
- **Supplement II: Guide to Using AI in Teaching in Primary and Secondary Schools** (pp.2-4 and 2-9): requires learning data to be handled properly, and the identifiable parts removed before student work is uploaded for AI marking.
- **EDBCM No. 156/2026** (2026/27 School Year Holistic Planning of School-based Digital Education and Related Teacher Training): states that schools must build digital education into the School Development Plan and/or the Annual School Plan in the 2026/27 school year.
- **EDBCM No. 107/2026** (Training on Implementing Artificial Intelligence Education Planning in Schools and Teacher Professional Training in Artificial Intelligence): sets out the training arrangements for a school planning AI education.

**Learning strand**: fundamental operating principles of AI / human-AI communication and collaboration / reflection on the application of AI
