> A rubric that scores the finished piece teaches pupils to improve the piece. This one records two things side by side — what is finished, and how the thinking went — and never adds them together.

## What this solves

Most rubrics reward the artefact. A neat poster, a complete table, a tidy model: the marks go where the polish is, and a pupil learns quickly that the fastest route to a good result is a better-looking file. What the pupil actually did — what they asked, what they checked, what they changed and why — leaves no trace, so it cannot be noticed, praised or taught.

This page separates the two. One column records only which parts of the work are finished. The other describes four process items in words. They sit side by side on one sheet, and they are never added into a total — because a pupil who produced a thin artefact through careful checking and a pupil who produced a full one by copying are not the same case, and a single number cannot say so.

The description is done twice: the pupil first describes their own process, then reads the teacher's description of the same four items, and circles only where the two differ. Nobody changes a description to match the other. The differences are the useful part of the lesson.

**Three things to read first:**

1. **The four process items and the four descriptions are this resource's suggestion.** The Education Bureau's documents do not name them and do not prescribe a rubric of this shape; what they do ask for is professional oversight of AI-generated teaching content and the cultivation of critical thinking, and this sheet is one way of recording both without a score.
2. **Nothing on this sheet is scored.** There is no mark, no percentage, no grade and no weighting anywhere in the sheet or on this page; the descriptions are words about one piece of work, not a judgement of a pupil. No conversation record, screenshot, tool output or account detail is ever collected.
3. **Every sample is fictional.** The worked example below is invented to show the format; no real pupil, class or school appears in it, and no brand, model or supplier is named anywhere — a tool, where one is mentioned at all, is "the tool the school has designated".

The sheet is about one piece of work. It is not a report on a pupil, and it does not travel into a mark book.

## Learning objectives

By the end, pupils can:

1. Say what each of the two columns is for, and why the two are never added together.
2. Fill the artefact column by listing which required parts are finished and which are not, without judging how good they are.
3. Describe their own work on each of the four process items, using one of the four descriptions and one line of evidence from the sheet or the work.
4. Read a teacher's description of the same four items and circle only the places where it differs from their own.
5. Leave both descriptions as written, and say what the differences tell them about their next piece of work.

The page addresses **human-machine communication and collaboration** and **reflection on the application of AI**: the work may well have been produced with a tool, and the process column is where the pupil says what they asked it, what they checked and what they changed. Three values are practised: **critical thinking** is being able to say where a claim came from; **responsibility** is describing your own process honestly when nobody is checking; **using technology at the right time** is being able to show that a tool was used as a step, not as the whole job.

## How to use it

One 60-minute lesson in six parts: the two columns, the artefact column, the four process items, the four descriptions, self-description and circling, and the close. The items are four and the descriptions are four; do not add a fifth, do not merge two, and keep the artefact column first.

### 1 One sheet, two columns, fixed

The sheet has two columns and no total row. The left column is the artefact; the right column is the process. Both describe the same piece of work, on the same sheet, on the same day.

- **The artefact column is filled first.** It is quicker, and it settles what the piece of work is before anyone starts talking about how it was made.
- **No total, no weighting, no combined line.** There is deliberately nowhere on the sheet to put one; if a school wants a record for its own purposes, that record is kept elsewhere and is not this sheet.
- **One piece of work per sheet.** Not a portfolio, not a term's output: one piece, so that the four descriptions can point at something specific.

### 2 The artefact column: completion only

This column answers one question — which required parts of the work are finished — and nothing else.

| What is written | What is not written |
|---|---|
| The parts the task asked for, one per line, marked finished or not finished | Whether the finished part is good, neat, detailed or accurate |
| A word about what is still missing, in the pupil's own words | A number, a percentage, a tick count or a proportion |

- **"Finished" means present, not good.** A rough paragraph counts as finished if it is there; a beautiful heading does not count as a finished section.
- **The column can be short.** Three or four lines is normal for a single piece of work; a long list usually means the task was really several tasks.

### 3 The four process items, fixed

Four items, in this order. Each one is described from what is visible in the work or on the sheet, never from what the pupil says they usually do.

| Item | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| Asking | What question did you set out to answer, and why that one rather than a wider one? |
| Checking | What did you check, where did you check it, and what did you find? |
| Revising | What did you change, and what made you change it? |
| Explaining why | Why is the work the way it is — what did you decide, and on what grounds? |

- **A tool's answer is not a check.** "I asked the tool and it said so" belongs under Asking, not under Checking; Checking needs a source outside the conversation.
- **"I did not check anything" is an answer.** It is described as it is; the sheet is not a trap and there is no penalty line.

### 4 The four descriptions, fixed

Every item gets one of four descriptions. They are words about one piece of work, not grades, and they are not converted into anything.

| Description | What is visible on the sheet |
|---|---|
| Not seen | Nothing on the sheet or in the work shows this item at all. |
| Beginning | Something is there — a word, a mark, a one-line note — but it does not yet say what was done or why. |
| Clear | It is written so that another pupil could read it and say what was done and what decided it. |
| Could teach it | It is written so clearly that the pupil could use it to show someone else how to do the same. |

- **The description is about the writing, not the pupil.** "Clear" describes a line on a sheet; it does not describe a person's ability.
- **A high description is not a reward.** A pupil may be "Could teach it" on Revising and "Not seen" on Checking in the same piece of work; that combination is the most useful thing the sheet can show.

### 5 Self-description first, then circle only the differences

The pupil describes the four items first. Then the teacher's description of the same four items is read against it.

- **The pupil writes first**, in pencil, using the four descriptions and one line of evidence for each item.
- **The teacher describes the same four items separately**, without seeing the pupil's column where the school prefers it that way; the two are then laid side by side.
- **Where the two differ, the pupil circles the difference** with a coloured pencil — a circle, not a correction. Nobody changes a description, and there is no score to change.
- **Agreement needs no mark.** Where the two match, nothing is written; the circled places are the whole record of the conversation.

### 6 The 60-minute lesson

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 8 minutes | The two columns: what each is for, and why there is no total |
| 10 minutes | The artefact column: list the parts, mark finished or not finished |
| 12 minutes | The four items: read them, and find one line of evidence for Asking and Checking |
| 10 minutes | Describe all four items in pencil, one description and one line each |
| 12 minutes | Read the teacher's description, circle only the differences |
| 8 minutes | Close: each pupil names one difference and one thing to do differently next time |

**Three closing lines** (the class reads them together):

1. "This part is finished; that part is not."
2. "This is what I checked, and where."
3. "Here is where my description and my teacher's differ."

## Answer key and marking notes

**A worked sample (fictional, to show the format)**

| Artefact column | Process column |
|---|---|
| Introduction: finished. Data table: finished. Two-line conclusion: not finished. Sources list: not finished. | Asking — Clear: "I wanted to know whether the two year groups differed, because the table already showed a gap." Checking — Beginning: "I checked the second figure against the class copy." Revising — Clear: "I changed 'most pupils' to 'the pupils in my class', because that is who was asked." Explaining why — Not seen. |

**What a good description line looks like:** it names the action and points at where it can be seen — "I changed X to Y, because the sheet only had twelve replies" — rather than summarising the pupil's character.

**Common errors and what to say:**

- **The artefact column written as quality judgements.** Response: this column records finished or not finished; save the judgement, because there is nowhere on this sheet to put it.
- **"Checked: asked the tool again."** Response: that is Asking, not Checking; Checking needs something outside the conversation.
- **Descriptions written without evidence.** Response: one line per item, and the line must point at something in the work or on the sheet.
- **A pupil changing their description to match the teacher's.** Response: circle the difference and leave both as written; the difference is the record.

**What counts as done:** the artefact column lists the required parts as finished or not finished; all four items carry one description and one line of evidence; the differences from the teacher's description are circled and neither description is altered. Nothing is scored, and no total appears anywhere.
