> A pupil who can give the answer cannot necessarily give the idea. One sentence, three levels of follow-up question, three minutes, four columns — this is the step no tool can take for you.

## What this solves

A pupil copies the working out neatly and the answer is right. Ask "why this step?" and the reply is "because that is the formula". That is not a reason; it is recall. A tool can generate the steps, and it can generate a sentence that sounds like a reason. What it cannot answer is "what were you trying to do here", and "if the numbers changed, if a condition changed, would this step still hold".

This page does one thing: **take one sentence from one step of the lesson, put three levels of question to it, in three minutes, and record four columns.** It asks for no conversation to be handed over, no screenshot and no score. It asks the teacher to hear a pupil explain the idea in their own words, and to write that one sentence down.

Why a sentence and not a question: a question has a standard answer, and a pupil can remember the shape of the answer without having thought about the step. A sentence is put together by the pupil; if they can say it, they were thinking about that step at the time. So what is chosen is "I did it this way because...", not the sentence carrying the final answer, and not a step copied off the board.

Why three levels: the first asks for the idea, the second for the reason, the third for the what-if. Each level looks at a different thing — whether they thought about it, whether they can explain why, and whether they can carry the step into another situation. One level alone shows memory; three levels on the same sentence show where the thinking stops.

Read the page at three levels too. In **literacy and framework** it corresponds to "reflection on the application of AI". In **learning and teaching** it is a three-minute oral follow-up question inside a lesson, asking for a reason and for an explain. In **values** it exercises critical discernment and reflection: a neat piece of working is not evidence that the step was thought about.

## Purpose

By the end, a teacher can:

1. Take one sentence from a step of the lesson — "I did it this way because..." — rather than a question or the final answer.
2. Put three levels to that one sentence: the idea, the reason, the what-if, in that order.
3. Do one pupil in three minutes: one to speak, one for a group-mate to restate, one for the teacher to write four columns.
4. Take four to six pupils a lesson, one minute as the ceiling, and drop a pupil rather than cut the second minute.
5. Record "not answered" honestly, without teaching on the spot, without a second follow-up and without a score.
6. Treat the record as one that never enters a report card, is not an oral exam and compares no pupil with another; the tool is always "the tool the school has specified".

The material corresponds to the "reflection on the application of AI" strand of AI literacy, and to critical discernment: judging whether a sentence is a reason, not whether the working looks tidy. When a pupil can give a reason and reflect on it, no tool can take that step for them.

## How to use it

One 60-minute lesson, in six segments: 5 minutes to explain, 8 to demonstrate, 15 for group practice, 20 to take four to six pupils, 7 to close, 5 for the teacher to finish the record. **The order does not change**; if time runs short, cut the depth of the demonstration — never a level, never a column, and never the second minute.

| Time | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 5 min | Explain | State the one sentence, three levels, three minutes, four columns; state that no conversation is handed over, nothing is screenshotted and nothing is scored. |
| 8 min | Demonstrate | The teacher takes one fictional sentence through the three levels, and shows that "because that is the formula" is not a second-level answer. |
| 15 min | Group practice | Each group picks one sentence and puts the three levels to each other; the group listens once and comments on nothing. |
| 20 min | Four to six pupils | Three minutes each: one to speak, one for a group-mate to restate, one for the teacher to write. |
| 7 min | Close | Read out only the row numbers whose next step is "ask again", and say that the next lesson asks the third level only. |
| 5 min | Finish the record | Complete any unfinished column; a pupil who could not answer stays "not answered". |

### 1. One sentence, not one question

- Take one sentence from one step of the lesson and write it as "I did it this way because...".
- **Not the sentence with the final answer**: the answer is the result, not the step.
- **Not a step copied off the board**: a copied step can only be recited, not explained.
- One sentence is one sentence: no second sentence, no paragraph; the working and the method stay on the page and only that sentence is spoken.
- The pupil chooses it. If they choose the answer sentence, the teacher sends them back one step rather than choosing for them.

### 2. Three levels of follow-up question (three only)

| Level | Question | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — the idea | What were you trying to do here? | whether they thought about what the step was for |
| 2 — the reason | Why this step, and not the step before or after it? | whether they can give a reason rather than the name of a formula |
| 3 — the what-if | If the numbers changed, if a condition changed, would this step still hold? | whether they can carry the step into another situation |

All three levels go to **the same sentence**. The order does not change: the idea first, then the reason, and only then the condition. Reverse it and a pupil opens with "it would not work if the numbers changed" while the idea and the reason have never been said.

- Three levels only: no fourth level, and none dropped.
- "Because that is the formula" is not a second-level answer; after one follow-up, if the pupil still cannot answer, write "not answered".
- **One follow-up only, and nothing taught on the spot**: the teacher does not re-teach, does not demonstrate and does not supply the answer.
- The third level must not turn into a calculation: it asks whether this method still holds, not for the pupil to work the sum again.
- All three levels are recorded in the pupil's own words; filler words may be dropped, the pupil's phrasing is not rewritten.

### 3. The three-minute routine

Three minutes a pupil, timed. When the three minutes are up it stops; an unfinished column waits for the last five minutes.

| Minute | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Minute 1 | The pupil | Says one sentence — "I did it this way because..." — and takes the three levels in order |
| Minute 2 | One group-mate | Restates that sentence and the reason in their own words; no comment, no addition, no correction |
| Minute 3 | The teacher | Writes one row of the four-column record: pupil / the level reached / the one reason the pupil gave / the next step |

- **Four to six pupils** a lesson, one minute as the ceiling.
- **Drop a pupil rather than cut the second minute**: the restating is for the group, not for the teacher.
- One pupil is taken at most once across four consecutive lessons; the order is set by the teacher beforehand and does not follow hands up.
- One row only; the next step is one of three: ask again / take another sentence / nothing further.
- A pupil who cannot answer is written as "not answered" — not "does not understand", and not "incomplete".

### 4. Six things the teacher holds

1. **The record only, never the conversation**: no chat record, screenshot, account detail or pupil's own draft is handed over, uploaded or photocopied.
2. **No score, no oral assessment, no report card**: no level, no rank, and no comparison with a classmate.
3. **Nothing taught on the spot**: one follow-up only; if the pupil cannot answer, write "not answered" and leave the teaching to the next lesson.
4. **No tool, platform or supplier is judged**: the whole sheet says "the tool the school has specified", with no brand, model, supplier, institution, country or city named.
5. **Nothing written as a Bureau requirement**: the three levels, three minutes, four columns and the 60-minute shape are this resource's suggestion, and a school may adjust them to its classes.
6. **Every example is fictional**: the sentence used to demonstrate is written by the teacher, never a real pupil's words, work or draft.

### 5. Closing and follow-up

The close does one thing: read out the row numbers whose next step is "ask again" — the numbers only, no name and no content — and say that the next lesson asks the third level of the same sentence.

- The next lesson asks **the third level only** of the same sentence: if the numbers changed, if a condition changed, would this step still hold?
- Levels one and two are not asked again; a pupil already recorded is not taken again to repeat them.
- Follow-up one: put the same sentence at the start of the next lesson and give one minute to a different pupil for the third level.
- Follow-up two: after four lessons, read out the number of "not answered" rows and change one question, not the three levels.

The teacher does not verify the mathematics or correct the answer on the spot; the aim is to make the reasoning audible, so that over-reliance on a single worked answer becomes visible in the student's own words.

## Review checklist

Run through it once before the lesson. If any line does not hold, change the practice, not the record.

- □ What was chosen is one sentence — "I did it this way because..." — not a question and not the final answer.
- □ All three levels are present, in the order: the idea, the reason, the what-if.
- □ All three levels go to the same sentence, with no swap halfway.
- □ Three minutes a pupil: one to speak, one to restate, one to write; the second minute was not cut.
- □ Four to six pupils were taken, and no pupil more than once across four lessons.
- □ All four columns are present: pupil, the level reached, the one reason given, the next step.
- □ A pupil who could not answer is recorded as "not answered", with nothing taught on the spot and no second follow-up.
- □ Nothing anywhere asks for a conversation, a screenshot, account detail or a pupil's draft.
- □ Nothing is scored, levelled or assessed orally, and nothing is ranked; the record does not enter a report card.
- □ No brand, model, supplier, institution, country or city is named; the tool is always "the tool the school has specified".
- □ Nothing is written as a Bureau requirement.

> The core is **one sentence, three levels, three minutes, four columns.** If the third level can be answered, the page has done its work; if the only answer is "because that is the formula", it was a recitation.
