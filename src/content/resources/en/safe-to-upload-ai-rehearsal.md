## What this solves

Teachers want to hand worksheets, reports or class photos to an AI tool for marking or organising, but these files often contain **pupils' names, student numbers, class, contact details, photos, or medical / special educational needs records**. Once uploaded, this information can leave the school's control.

This exercise does not lecture on abstract principles; instead it lets teachers **practise once, hands-on**: find the identifiable information in six fictional documents, remove it, and then judge what risks remain.

## Learning objectives

By the end, teachers can:

1. Identify the elements of school documents that count as "identifiable personal information" (not just names).
2. Use the three techniques "remove → replace → blur" to turn a document into a version that is safe to upload.
3. Judge the residual risks that **remain** after names have been removed (for example a combination of class + date + a special event).
4. State the three checks to carry out before uploading.

## Teaching steps

1. **Introduction (5 min)** — Ask: "If you were going to hand a pupil's composition to an AI for marking, what would you do first?" Collect the answers without judging them.
2. **Demonstration (5 min)** — Project "Example 1" and demonstrate removing information step by step, explaining what is removed at each step and why.
3. **Group work (20 min)** — Each group works on two examples (chosen from Examples 2–6) and fills in the "before-you-upload checklist".
4. **Whole-class checking (10 min)** — Check against the answer key in the attachments. Focus the discussion on "what risk remains after names are removed".
5. **Wrap-up (5 min)** — Together, write out a three-step checking prompt that the school can use.

## Answer key — key ideas

- **A name is not the only identifier.** Class + seat number, class + the date and nature of an event, and rare combinations (for example "the school's only non-Chinese-speaking new arrival"), are all enough to identify an individual pupil.
- **Photos carry higher risk than text.** A uniform, the classroom background, a name badge, or another pupil's face can all amount to identification.
- **"Removed" does not mean "safe".** Ask: once the remaining pieces of information are combined, who else in the school could still recognise the pupil?
- **Marking does not require identity.** If the AI is only being asked to mark, a pupil's name is of no use to the AI and should always be removed.

## Before-you-upload checklist (can be posted in the staff room)

```
□ Name, student number, class, seat number — removed or replaced with "Student A"
□ Contact details (phone, email, home address) — removed
□ Photo / video — removed, or cropped so no identifiable background remains
□ Special educational needs, medical, discipline or counselling records — removed
□ The combination of date + class + event — checked (the combination itself can identify)
□ File name / metadata — checked (file names often contain a name)
□ Do I really need to upload this file? — confirmed as needed for the purpose
```

## How this relates to the EDB framework

This resource maps to **G (the Guide to Using AI in Teaching in Primary and Secondary Schools, pp.2-4 and 2-9)**: the document requires learning data to be handled properly, and for identifiable parts to be removed before pupil work is uploaded for AI marking.
