## What this solves

The most common way AI goes wrong in a maths lesson is a pupil asking one question and getting the whole worked solution with the answer in a single reply — the thinking stops the moment the question is asked. The teacher marks a "correct" answer but cannot see whether the pupil understood it or where they stalled.

These cards turn the AI from an answer-giver into a hint-giver: the teacher writes a controlled prompt first, the AI replies with one hint at the level the teacher asked for, and the teacher decides when and how far to release it. The thinking stays with the pupil, and the stall becomes visible to the teacher as a lead for the next lesson.

The cards can sit inside a school's digital education planning as a classroom-observation, formative assessment and feedback tool for AI + Subject in a maths lesson: the teacher sets a controlled prompt, and how far the questioning may go is the teacher's call.

## Purpose

Once prepared, the teacher can:

1. Write a "hint, not answer" controlled prompt and spot when the AI's reply sneaks in a value or the final answer.
2. Release hints level by level (reread the question, then strategy direction, then the first step, then compare and check) instead of giving the direction all at once.
3. Use the hint record to note the first level at which each pupil stalled, and see where the class as a whole stalled.
4. Use the record to plan the next lesson's focus, rather than guessing from the answers they marked.

The approach covers the human-AI communication strand of AI literacy, leaving the decision with the teacher and keeping timely and appropriate use and human-AI collaboration at the centre.

## How to use it

Preparation takes about 30 minutes before the lesson; the cards then work in any practice lesson. The before-lesson flow:

1. **Write the controlled prompts (10 min)** — Write a "hint only" prompt template for the day's question type (see the example below).
2. **Print the cards (5 min)** — Print the four-level cards and the topic cards you need, plus the hint record.
3. **Set up the hint record (5 min)** — Fill in the class and topic, and keep a column for "first level at which they stalled".
4. **Test-run once (10 min)** — Using a fictional question, check that the AI stops at a hint and gives no answer; adjust the prompt if needed.

In the lesson (time depends on the practice): hand out L1 first; only move a pupil up to L2, L3, then L4 when they are stuck. Each time you move up, note in the record the level where the pupil first stalled.

### Four hint levels

| Level | Name | It may contain | It may not contain |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Reread the question | Restate what the question asks; name the quantity being found | Any method, any value |
| **L2** | Strategy direction | Name the method or theorem to consider | Substitution, any working step, any value |
| **L3** | First step | The first algebraic or logical step only | Any later step, the final value |
| **L4** | Compare and check | Compare two workable methods and say which is more efficient and why | The answer |

### Turning an "answer me" prompt into a "hint me" prompt

**Weak prompt (pupil / uncontrolled):** "Solve this quadratic equation for me, with all the steps and the answer."

**Controlled prompt (teacher):** "My pupil is stuck on this question. Do one thing only: restate what the question asks and name the quantity to be found. Do not mention any method, do not substitute, and give no value and no final answer."

A controlled prompt has three parts: **fix the level** (one thing only), **name what is banned** (no method / no value / no answer), and **do none of the working for the pupil**.

## Review checklist

### Before-you-use checklist

| Check | Passes when |
|---|---|
| The card names a method, not a value | Every card names only a method or step; no value that could be an answer appears anywhere |
| The four levels are distinct | L1 only restates the question; L2 only names a method; L3 only the first step; L4 only compares methods |
| The prompt has all three parts | Every controlled prompt fixes the level, names what is banned, and does no working |
| The record is ready | It has a column for the pupil and for "first level at which they stalled" |
| It has been test-run | A fictional question was run once and the AI gave no answer |

### Hint record

| Pupil | Question / type | First level they stalled | Last level used | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (example) | Maximum of a quadratic function | L2 | L3 | Finished once the method was clear |

> The example is fictional and involves no real pupil or school.

### What to do with the record next lesson

- If most of the class stalls at **L1**: reteach reading the question, before teaching any method.
- If most stalls at **L2**: they read the question but cannot choose a method — next lesson focus on "spot the method".
- If only a few pupils stall at **L3 or L4**: follow up individually or in a small group, without slowing the class.
- The record is also **formative**: it shows where thinking stops, not a mark.
