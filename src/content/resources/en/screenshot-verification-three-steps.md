> A screenshot may be untouched, or one line of it may have been changed. The question is not whether it is true. The question is **whether it has yet met the conditions for being passed on.**

## What this solves

Screenshots are the most-forwarded material in a school: a notice, a fragment of a conversation, a line saying "reply before noon today". It looks like evidence — there is a picture, a time, some dialogue — but by the third or fourth hand nobody knows where it first appeared.

The difficulty is not that pupils are gullible. It is that **there is no point at which anyone is expected to stop.** Sharing takes a second; asking "where did this come from" needs a routine. This page is that routine: three steps, five columns, four situations, one lesson.

The three steps settle one question only: **does this screenshot now meet the conditions for being shared?** They do not judge whether the content is true, and they do not ask a pupil to work out whether an image was generated. That is not something a classroom can do, and it should not be handed to pupils as a task.

**Three things to read first:**

1. **The names "three steps", "five-column check sheet" and "the four situations" are this resource's suggestion; the Education Bureau's documents do not use them.** The basis is what those documents do require: **checking and verifying information**, **not using or forwarding unverified information casually**, and **protecting personal data**. No document prescribes the number of steps, the columns or a lesson sequence.
2. **This page offers no tool for deciding whether something is real, and it passes no judgement on any platform.** The steps test the conditions for sharing; they do not test truth. Pupils are not asked to decide whether an image was generated, and no detection method is taught. The four situations explain only why a screenshot reads as genuine — there is no technical analysis.
3. **The Primary Information and Technology Curriculum Framework is a primary pilot.** This page covers P4-S6; the secondary years use the same vocabulary, but no equivalent official framework exists for them, so nothing here is presented as a secondary requirement and none of its items are quoted.

**This page does not argue that "no screenshot can be trusted", and it does not argue that "seeing is believing".** Neither is true: a screenshot can be genuine, and it can also merely look genuine. There is one conclusion — **until it has been verified, do not pass it on as fact.**

## Learning objectives

By the end, pupils can:

1. State the three steps, in order, and what each one is trying to find.
2. Try to trace a screenshot to its original, and **stop at step one** when the original cannot be found.
3. Compare a screenshot with the original line by line, and name any person, date, number or condition that has been cropped away.
4. Find a second source unconnected to the post, and explain why two items that quote each other count as one.
5. Record a decision on the five-column sheet, and accept that "do not share" is a completed answer.

The page addresses **reflection on the application of AI**, one of the three learning strands, and sits between **checking and verifying information** and **protecting personal data**. Three values are practised at once: **critical thinking** means not treating "it looks like it matches" as "it matches"; **responsibility** means thinking once about how the person receiving the picture will use it; **using technology at the right time** means not turning this routine into a formality in situations that need no verification at all.

## How to use it

There are three steps and no more. The order is fixed — not swapped, not merged, nothing added. Each step asks one question, and the next step begins only when that question has an answer. If a step cannot be answered, the process stops there and nothing is shared.

### Step 1: Find the original

Establish **where the screenshot first appeared, on what date, and who published it.** All three. If one of them cannot be written down, the original has not been found.

- **No original, no sharing — the process stops here.** This is not a penalty. Without the original there is nothing for step two to compare against.
- **"I only found reposts" counts as "no".** The same picture posted again in another group, by another person, is still a repost.
- The original may be a notice that has been published, a document that has been uploaded, or something the person concerned published themselves. It does **not** have to be something found online: the copy obtained through the school's own official channel is an original too.

### Step 2: Does it match?

Put the screenshot beside the original and ask two things of every line:

- **Is there an identical sentence in the original?** A similar meaning is not a match; the words have to line up.
- **Has a name, date, number or condition been cropped away?** Remove one "except", one "only", one "must first", and the sentence says the opposite.

**Three prompts — prompts only, never a basis for a decision:**

1. Uneven edges.
2. Inconsistent type.
3. Words on the same line sitting at different heights.

These three tell you to read it again and compare it again. They are not evidence and cannot settle anything: a genuine screenshot can have uneven edges, and an altered one can be perfectly tidy. **This page teaches no detection tool and lists no technical indicators.**

### Step 3: A second source

Find **one source unconnected to the post.**

- **Two items that quote each other count as one source.** If both are describing the same thing and one of them credits the other, it is one source, not two.
- **Only when all three steps are answered may anything be shared.** And when it is shared, **the source and its date go with it**: say where the original is and when it was published, so the next person can go and look. Passing on the picture alone hands the work of checking to somebody else.

### The five-column check sheet (fixed at five)

One page, five columns, one per pupil. **No marks, no ranking, no levels.**

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| 1 What I received | One sentence: what kind of screenshot it is and where it came from (no group names, no people's names) |
| 2 Did I find the original | Where it is, the date, the publisher. Reposts only = "no" |
| 3 Does it match | Matches / does not match / not compared (name the sentence or item that differs) |
| 4 A second source | One source unconnected to the post; "none" if there is none |
| 5 My decision | Share (with source and date) / do not share (say which step you stopped at) |

Two lines must appear on the page and on the sheet:

- **"Do not share" is a completed answer.** Stopping at step one, two or three is finished work, not failure.
- **"I only found reposts" is treated as "no".** Column 2 says no, and there is nothing to guess at further down.

### The four situations (fixed at four)

These four explain why a screenshot reads as genuine. They are not four techniques, and all four go through the same three steps.

| Situation | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| Context cropped away | The picture is real but only one part of it survives, and the part that was removed changes the meaning |
| An old picture reused | The picture is real and the date is old; with new words attached it becomes "this happened today" |
| Real picture, added words | The image is real; the line printed across it was written later |
| A generated image | The whole picture came from a tool. It has no original, so step one fails at once |

### In class: pairs, two fictional screenshots

One 60-minute lesson. **Both screenshots are made by the teacher and entirely fictional**: no real school, person or class appears, there are no working links or QR codes, and no real conversation is captured. Every example on this page and in the attachments is fictional.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 10 min | **Demonstrate**: the teacher works the three steps on the first screenshot in front of the class, and stops at step two to show what "it does not match, so we stop" looks like |
| 15 min | **First screenshot, in pairs**: each pupil fills in the five columns, then the pair compares columns 2 and 3 |
| 15 min | **Second screenshot, in pairs**: a fresh one, deliberately built so that only reposts can be found, so pupils practise writing "no" in column 2 |
| 10 min | **Peer review**: pairs swap and check only columns 3 and 5 — is the mismatch written down, does the decision say which step it stopped at |
| 10 min | **Close**: the class reads the three closing sentences together, and the teacher adds one line — so when we do share, the source travels with it |

**Three points of teacher control:**

1. **The teacher makes the screenshots.** Never taken from a real conversation or a real group, and no real screenshot is used; every example is fictional.
2. **Pupils are not asked to judge whether it is real.** The teacher does not ask "was this one generated", and compares no tools; discussion stays inside what the three steps can answer.
3. **Personal data stops the exercise.** **Personal data in a screenshot must be covered**: no name, face, phone number, class or student number is copied onto the check sheet. If a screenshot involves personal data, harassment or a privacy matter, it goes to the teacher and is handled under the school's existing arrangements rather than discussed in class.

**Three closing sentences** (read together):

1. "I could not find the original."
2. "It does not match."
3. "So I am not sharing it."

## Answer key and marking notes

**One completed example (entirely fictional, to show the format)**

| 1 What I received | 2 Did I find the original | 3 Does it match | 4 A second source | 5 My decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A screenshot headed "activity time changed", forwarded by a classmate | Original: the school notice board, on a stated date, published by the activity coordinator | Does not match: the screenshot says "all year groups", the original says "S3 to S6" | Yes: the same notice on the school's official channel | Share, with the location and date of the original |
| A screenshot headed "fee notice", forwarded by a relative | No: only reposts in other groups could be found | Not compared | None | Do not share (stopped at step one) |

**Three ways of stopping, all of them completed work:**

| Stopped at | What should be on the sheet | How the teacher responds |
|---|---|---|
| Step 1 | Column 2 says "no", column 5 says "do not share (stopped at step 1)" | "You have finished. With no original there is nothing to compare." |
| Step 2 | Column 3 names the sentence that does not match, column 5 says "do not share (stopped at step 2)" | "The difference you found matters. Say it so the class can hear it." |
| Step 3 | Column 4 says "none", column 5 says "do not share (stopped at step 3)" | "One source is not enough. That is the rule, not your problem." |

**Common wrong answers:**

- **A repost treated as the original.** Column 2 names a location, but it is a repost in another group. Ask: "is that where it was first published?"
- **The three prompts used as a verdict.** "The edges are uneven, so it is fake." The prompts only send you back to compare; the decision still rests on the original and a second source.
- **Judging whether it is real.** "This was definitely generated." This page does not make that judgement; what has to be written is which step the process reached.
- **Sharing after a mismatch.** Column 5 says "share" while column 3 says "does not match". All three steps have to be answered — not most of them.

**What counts as finished**: all five columns filled in (columns 3 and 4 may say "none" or "not compared"), and column 5 either "share, with source and date" or "do not share, stopped at step N". The sheet is a record of what was done. It is **not marked, not ranked**, and never becomes a conduct record.
