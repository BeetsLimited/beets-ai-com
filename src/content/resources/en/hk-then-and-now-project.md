## What this solves

"Hong Kong then and now" is a popular local history project, but the usual version asks every pupil to go home and interview a relative. Not every pupil can or should do that: pupils from newly arrived or cross-border families may have relatives unfamiliar with old Hong Kong, and looked-after children, pupils who have been bereaved, and families with language barriers can all find the task difficult or distressing.

This resource turns "interview a relative" from a **requirement** into **one of two paths**. Pupils who can and want to interview someone use the interview prompts; everyone else uses the public-source path, marked exactly the same, and completes the project from street views, old maps, old newspapers and library records. It also includes a worked source check and a three-level rubric.

## Learning objectives

By the end, pupils can:

1. Choose a local place and name at least one specific difference between its past and its present.
2. Use one of two paths — interviewing a relative or using public sources — to gather information about the place.
3. Check an AI's claim about the place against a real source and say what is right, wrong or unverified.
4. Explain that the project can be completed without interviewing family, and respect classmates' different situations.

## Teaching steps

1. **Introduction (5 min)** — Show two images of the same place, old and new (a diagram is fine), and ask: "What has changed here, and how do you know?"
2. **Demonstrate checking (10 min)** — Use the worked source check in the attachment to show how an AI's claim about "Banyan Street" is checked against an old map and an old newspaper, and how the right and wrong parts are found.
3. **Choose a path (5 min)** — Introduce the two paths: Path A interviewing a relative, Path B public sources. Stress that both paths count and are marked identically.
4. **Group planning (10 min)** — Pupils choose a place and a path and fill in the planning section of the worksheet; the teacher circulates to make sure everyone has a workable starting point.
5. **Wrap-up and set the task (5-10 min)** — Explain the task and the "three never" rules (see teacher notes), and remind pupils to bring one source next lesson.

## Answer key and marking notes

**A good open answer** is not "it is different now"; it must name a **specific difference** (e.g. "it used to be a pier, now it is a park") and **where that came from** (whom they interviewed / which map or record).

**Worked source check (fictional)**: the task is "we check an AI's claim about the past of Banyan Street".

| AI's claim | Source checked | Result |
|---|---|---|
| There was a market at one end | A 1973 old map (fictional) | Correct: the map shows a market at one end |
| There was a cinema in the middle | The same map + an old newspaper (fictional) | Partly wrong: the map shows a small open-air theatre, and the newspaper calls it "the Banyan Street theatre" |
| The cinema closed in 1985 and became a shopping centre | A library newspaper index (fictional) | Unverified: the index has no record of a 1985 closure |

Conclusion: the AI's answer is partly right and partly wrong, so it cannot be trusted without checking it against a source.

**Rubric (4 criteria × 3 levels)**:

| Criterion | Getting started | On target | Great |
|---|---|---|---|
| Describing the place | Only says "it changed", with no specific difference | Names at least one specific difference | Names several differences, grouped (buildings, transport, daily life) |
| Using sources | Cannot say where the information came from | Says the source (whom they interviewed / which map or record) | Uses more than one source and compares whether they agree |
| Checking the AI's claim | Trusts the AI without checking | Checks against one source and says what was right and wrong | Distinguishes right, wrong and "not verified", and explains how |
| Presenting and reflecting | Unclear, or reflection not done | Presents clearly and names one thing learned | Presents clearly, names what was learned and how to improve next time |

## Official documents

This resource maps to **AI Literacy Learning Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools (Examples of Application Scenarios)** (p.8): the document proposes a Hong Kong "past and present" project in which pupils use AI to help imagine the past, then check the AI's account against oral history and other sources. This resource turns that scenario into a worksheet every pupil can complete, with an added path that needs no family interview.
