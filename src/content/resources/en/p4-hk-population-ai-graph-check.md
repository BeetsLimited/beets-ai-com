> Classroom focus: **the number series must be verified against official Census and Statistics Department data** — do not take the AI's figures at face value.

## What this solves
Students will soon be using AI to get information. The real skill is **knowing to ask AI where its information comes from — and actually checking it**.

This lesson uses a topic close to everyday life (Hong Kong's population) to let students discover for themselves that an AI explanation is **partly right and partly wrong**, then get the data from an official source and redraw the correct line graph.

The lesson can sit inside a school's digital education planning and run in a maths lesson, showing how to check a figure an AI gives, with official data as the basis for assessment; the part of the explanation that is wrong is an AI hallucination the pupils catch for themselves, and the only way to remove it is to verify and fact-check it against the official figures.

## Learning objectives
Students will be able to:

1. Ask AI where its information comes from, and judge whether the answer names a source.
2. Compare each figure the AI provides against the official Census and Statistics Department data.
3. Point out **which statement** in the AI explanation is wrong, and explain why using evidence.
4. Redraw the line graph using the official data, and describe the trend.

Written for the upper-primary learning stage, it covers the reflection on the application of AI strand of AI literacy, building critical thinking and keeping human judgement first.

## How to use it
1. **Hook (5 minutes)** — Project "the AI's explanation" (see the download), and ask: can this be trusted? How would you check it?
2. **Set up a checking method (5 minutes)** — Together, list the checking steps: find the source → compare the figures → check whether each claim is supported by the data.
3. **Group checking (15 minutes)** — Students check each statement using the official Census and Statistics Department data.
4. **Redraw the line graph (10 minutes)** — Plot the graph using the official data, and describe the trend in one sentence.
5. **Wrap up (5 minutes)** — Discuss: if the AI got half its figures right, why is it still risky?

## Answer key and marking notes
The AI explanation has three problems:

1. **No source.** It never says where any of its figures come from — this is the first warning sign.
2. **The claim that it "rises every year" does not match the data.** Hong Kong's population does not rise in a single direction year after year; after checking the official data, students will find years in which it fell. This is the lesson's most important discovery.
3. **The figures for future years have no basis.** The AI presents an estimate as a fact, confusing "what has happened" with "a forecast".

Also draw students' attention to this: **the part the AI got right (the 2021 population figure) does not make the whole passage trustworthy.** A passage that is half right can still mislead.
